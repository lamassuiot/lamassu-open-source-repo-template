# Legal — Contributor License Agreements

This directory governs the Contributor License Agreements (CLAs) used by this repository.
This process is currently **manual**: there is no automated CLA bot or GitHub App. A maintainer
verifies and records CLA coverage by hand for each pull request (see
[cla-maintainer-runbook.md](./cla-maintainer-runbook.md)).

> **Status:** The agreement text in [ICLA.md](./ICLA.md) and [CCLA.md](./CCLA.md) is a
> **legal-review draft**. It is not yet approved by Lamassu's legal counsel and must not be
> treated as a final, binding agreement until that review is complete. See the "Legal-review
> status" field in each document.

## Purpose

The Individual Contributor License Agreement (ICLA) and Corporate Contributor License Agreement
(CCLA) exist to give the project a clear, documented license (and, where applicable, patent
grant) from each contributor to the code, documentation, and other material they contribute.
This protects both contributors and downstream users of the project by removing ambiguity about
what rights accompany a contribution.

## When each agreement applies

- **ICLA** — applies when you are contributing on your own behalf, and your employer (if any)
  has no rights to your contribution.
- **CCLA** — applies when you are contributing as an employee, contractor, or representative of
  a company, and that company may have rights to your contribution. A CCLA is signed by someone
  authorized to bind the company, and it must identify (or otherwise make identifiable) the
  employees/contractors who are authorized to contribute under it.

Every contributor **and every co-author** listed on a pull request (for example, via
`Co-authored-by` trailers) must be covered by a signed ICLA or a CCLA that includes them, before
that pull request can be merged.

## Requesting an ICLA (individuals)

1. Contact the maintainers at the address below and state that you are requesting an ICLA.
2. You will be sent the current version of the ICLA (see [ICLA.md](./ICLA.md)) to review and
   sign.
3. Return the signed agreement through the private channel provided by the maintainers — **not**
   as a public issue, pull request, or comment.
4. Once recorded, a maintainer will confirm your coverage privately.

## Requesting a CCLA (companies)

1. An authorized representative of the company contacts the maintainers at the address below and
   states that the company is requesting a CCLA.
2. The company will be sent the current version of the CCLA (see [CCLA.md](./CCLA.md)) to review
   and sign, along with guidance on how to list or otherwise identify the employees/contractors
   covered by it.
3. Return the signed agreement, and any evidence of the signatory's authority to bind the
   company, through the private channel provided by the maintainers — **not** as a public issue,
   pull request, or comment.
4. Once recorded, a maintainer will confirm coverage for the company's contributors privately.

## Where signed agreements are stored

Signed agreements, the list of individuals and companies covered, legal names, signatures, and
any supporting contracts or corporate-authorization evidence are stored in a **private**
registry maintained by Lamassu — they are **never** committed to this public repository, and
never included in issues, pull requests, or comments. This repository only contains the
unsigned, public agreement text and the manual verification process.
